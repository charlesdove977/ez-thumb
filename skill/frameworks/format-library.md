## Purpose
The named thumbnail formats, merged from every source in `creator-consensus.md`. Reference it when choosing what a concept is structurally, before writing a single prompt.

The single most important rule in this file is not any individual format. It is that **viral thumbnails combine two or three of them.** One format repeated is a template. Two or three combined is a concept.

---

## Core Concepts

### The library

| Format | What it is | Carries |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Simple** | One object, nothing else, on a clean ground | KISS, confidence |
| **Question** | The frame poses something the video answers | Question lever |
| **Shocking moment** | The unexpected instant, mid-event | Shock Factor |
| **Big numbers** | A number large enough to be the subject | Unknown Knowledge |
| **Size difference** | Two things at impossible relative scale | Shock Factor |
| **Comparison** | Before against after, or A against B | Progression |
| **Header** | A text bar giving context above the frame | Supporting device |
| **Blurred** | The result hidden behind a blur | Cliffhanger |
| **Weird object** | A thing that does not belong here | Question |
| **Social hacking** | A face or mark already known in the niche | Familiarity bias |
| **Close-up on the end result** | Tight crop on the payoff object | Unknown Knowledge |
| **Result** | The desired outcome shown plainly | Aspiration |

### Combining is the whole trick

A worked example from the corpus: one frame running **big numbers + header + comparison + social hacking** at once. Four formats, one composition, and it still holds a single focal point because the formats stack rather than compete.

How to stack without breaking KISS:

- **One format is the carrier.** It owns the main character.
- **The others are supporting elements or attention directors.** They give the carrier context.
- If two formats both want to be the subject, one of them is not in this thumbnail.

### Direction rules that are not optional

**Before goes on the left. After goes on the right. Always.**

The viewer reads the frame in reading order. A reversed pair inverts your story and the frame reads as a downgrade. Mixing it is one of the few genuinely serious errors in thumbnail design, because it is invisible to the designer and instant to the viewer.

### Choosing formats against the corpus

Do not pick from this table in a vacuum. Cross it against the operator's own corpus first:

1. Group the corpus by `concept_carrier` and rank on breakout
2. The top two carriers tell you which formats already work in this niche
3. The format appearing in almost every thumbnail regardless of performance is the **cliche**, and it is the one to break

## Examples

**Good stack.** Comparison as carrier, header as supporting element, big number inside the after panel. Three formats, one focal point, the number sits inside the winning half so it does not compete for attention.

**Bad stack.** Big numbers as carrier plus a shocking moment plus a weird object. Three subjects, three focal points, the eye picks none and scrolls.

**Good break of a cliche.** The niche runs comparison on nine of ten top thumbnails. You run a single weird object on a flat ground. It is the only frame on the page that does not look like the others.

**Bad break of a cliche.** The niche runs comparison because comparison is genuinely what the audience wants from this topic, and you avoid it purely to be different. Breaking the cliche is a bet on attention, not a rule; it has to still communicate the video.

## Anti-Patterns

| Pattern | Why it fails |
| --- | --- |
| One format, repeated every upload | Recognition collapses into interchangeability |
| Three formats all fighting to be the subject | Multiple focal points, the eye lands nowhere |
| Copying a format because a big channel used it | It may be their cliche too. Check your corpus first |
| Recreate-from-URL against a thumbnail in your own niche | You publish the eleventh instance of a frame the audience already saw at 100K, on a zero-view video |
| Before on the right | Inverts the story silently |
| Picking a format before the idea exists | Format is how the idea is carried, not a substitute for having one |

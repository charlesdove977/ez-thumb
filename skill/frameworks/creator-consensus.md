# Creator Consensus

What the best public thumbnail teardowns actually agree on, after cross-checking them against each other rather than taking any single one at face value.

Read it when a rule needs backing beyond this skill's own opinion, when a creator's advice contradicts something here, or when picking a format for a concept.

## The rules that survived the cross check

Each of these either holds in every source, or holds in one and is contradicted by none.

1. Complementary contrast between subject and background, verified in both light and dark mode
2. One focal point. Everything else supports it, directs attention to it, or gets deleted
3. Subject on a rule of thirds line
4. Bottom right kept clear for the duration stamp
5. Thumbnail text adds information the title does not carry. Bold sans serif, or nothing at all
6. One curiosity lever per frame, and the video pays it off
7. Real assets, shot flat and bright, cut out cleanly. Never a still pulled off the timeline
8. Formats are a library to combine from, two or three at once, not one template repeated
9. Research in-niche to find the cliche, take visual inspiration out-of-niche
10. Consistency in font and palette skeleton, rotation in accent and format about every fifth video
11. Build the thumbnail before the video, in variations
12. AI produces the mockup, real assets produce the final
13. Test before publishing and again after, because A/B outcomes swing both ways
14. Rank on retention-adjusted CTR, never CTR alone
15. A published thumbnail is not final. Bad CTR is a prompt to swap the frame, not a lost video

## The element hierarchy

The most precise version of the KISS rule in any of these sources, from DecodingYT. Every thumbnail has exactly three kinds of element:

- **a main character**, the one thing attention is meant to land on
- **supporting elements**, which give the main character context
- **attention directors**, arrows, gaze lines, gestures, pointing at the main character

Anything that is none of those three gets removed. His own worked example deleted a transformation arrow because the before and after labels were already carrying that story, which is the useful part: even an attention director gets cut when something else already does its job.

The stated mechanism, and it is worth carrying into every design decision:

> More elements equals less focus. Each additional element divides attention.

That is why the hierarchy deletes rather than rearranges.

## Contrast recipes

Three pairings stated as usable rather than as theory. Reach for these when the complementary wheel is not giving enough separation:

- black and white together
- highly saturated color on black
- white on a low saturation color

## Where the sources disagree

Worth knowing, because a rule everyone states differently is a rule to hold loosely.

**Thumbnail text length.** Sources range from "three words maximum" to "no text at all" to "text is fine if it adds information". Measured corpus data does not show word count predicting performance in either direction. **Resolution:** go short because the concept object should be carrying the idea, not because short words have power. If your text is doing all the work, the concept is missing.

**Faces.** Some sources treat a face as near-mandatory, others build entirely faceless. Both have real results behind them. **Resolution:** this is niche-specific and it moves within weeks. Measure it in your own corpus. See `frameworks/faceless-vs-face.md`.

**A/B testing.** Universally recommended, but the outcomes swing in both directions and small channels rarely have the impression volume to reach significance. **Resolution:** test when you have the traffic. When you do not, swapping a thumbnail on an underperforming video is still worth doing; it just is not a test, it is a fix.

## What measured data corrected

Two places where the popular advice is wrong often enough to call out.

**"Faceless wins" and "faces win" are both overstated.** Every confident percentage you have seen on this came from one niche in one window. See above.

**Raw view count is not evidence.** Almost every teardown ranks example thumbnails by view count. Across channels of different sizes that ranking is close to meaningless, and it is trivially poisoned by bought traffic. Rank on breakout and filter on like rate. See `frameworks/corpus-method.md`.
